\part{Historical Background}

% Lead-in to Part A: the county the Board governs, how it came to be a county,
% and why the history begins in 1870. The footnote names who governed the land
% before (docs/questions.csv, before-1870-governance).
The land this report follows was part of Fairfax County until 1801, when
Congress formed the Virginia side of the new federal district into the county
of Alexandria and governed it for 46 years through a federal circuit court and a
board of appointed
magistrates.\autocite{usstat1790residence}\fnsep\autocite[sec.~2]{usstat1801organic}\fnsep\autocite[secs.~1, 4 and 7]{usstat1801supplementary}
Congress returned the county to Virginia by an act of 1846, with the assent of
its voters at a poll that September, and Virginia's laws reached it in
1847.\autocite{usstat1846retrocession} Virginia's constitution of 1869 then
created a board of supervisors in every county, and Alexandria County, as
Arlington was called until 1920, elected its first three supervisors in May
1870.\autocite[art.~VII, sec.~2]{vaconstitution1869}\fnsep\autocite[chs.~39, 76 and 188]{vaacts1870}\fnsep\autocite[note~2]{forstall1996}
That Board is the subject of this report, and its history begins with that
election. It is not the first government to have ruled this ground, only the
one the report is about.\footnote{The Nacotchtank, whom the English also called
Necostins, lived on this shore of the Potomac when John Smith charted the river
in 1608; his map places a village, Nameroughquena, in what is now Arlington,
and by 1679 they had left it, to disease, war and the loss of their fishing,
hunting and farming grounds (\cite{arlingtonva2011markers};
\cite{nps2025rooseveltisland}). The Virginia colony and then Fairfax County governed the land until Congress
accepted a federal district on the Potomac in 1790, and the district's
Virginia side was a county of the District of Columbia from 1801 until Congress
returned it to Virginia.}
