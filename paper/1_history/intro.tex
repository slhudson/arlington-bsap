Following the Civil War, Virginia adopted a new state constitution in 1869,
which created a board of supervisors in every county, and that is where this
report begins.\autocite[art.~VII, sec.~2]{vaconstitution1869} The territory now
known as Arlington County was then called Alexandria County, having returned
to Virginia from the District of Columbia in 1847 after 46 years as the
District's county of Alexandria, which Congress governed through a circuit
court and appointed magistrates; before 1801 it had been part of Fairfax
County.\autocite[note~2]{forstall1996}\fnsep\autocite{usstat1790residence}\fnsep\autocite[sec.~2]{usstat1801organic}\fnsep\autocite[secs.~1, 4 and 7]{usstat1801supplementary}\fnsep\autocite{usstat1846retrocession}
The Board is the government this report is about, not the first to have ruled
this ground.\footnote{The Nacotchtank, whom the English also called Necostins,
lived on this shore of the Potomac when John Smith charted the river in 1608;
his map places a village, Nameroughquena, in what is now Arlington, and by 1679
they had left it, to disease, war and the loss of their fishing, hunting and
farming grounds (\cite{arlingtonva2011markers}; \cite{nps2025rooseveltisland}).
The Virginia colony and then Fairfax County governed the land until Congress
accepted a federal district on the Potomac in 1790, and the district's Virginia
side was a county of the District of Columbia from 1801 until Congress returned
it to Virginia.}
