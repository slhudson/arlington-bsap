\section*{Notes on the figures}

\subsection*{Seats}

Seats are counted in seat-years: a member who sat four months of a year counts
four twelfths of a seat, so a year in which one member replaced another shows
as half a seat each rather than a whole seat for both. The denominator is the
months the Board existed, which is twelve in every year but its first ---
Arlington's Board came into existence at the May 1870 election, so 1870 counts
eight months.

Two years fall below a full Board and both are real: 1873, when a district sat
vacant from June to November, and 1990, between a February resignation and a
May special election. 1931 is not reported in the source and is drawn as a gap
rather than a zero.

Race and gender are two splits of the same seats, drawn on the same scale, so
the two Board figures can be read against each other.

\subsection*{Population}

Population is the decennial census count for the territory the Board governed
in each year. Alexandria city has been independent since 1871 and is reported
separately from 1900; Alexandria annexed 866 acres in 1915 and a further tract
in 1930, so part of the movement between the 1910 and 1930 censuses is
territory changing hands rather than people arriving or leaving.

\subsection*{Race and ethnicity}

From 1980 the groups are the Census Bureau's race categories crossed with
Hispanic origin. They do not overlap and account for the county exactly:
Hispanic or Latino is of any race, and every other group is non-Hispanic.
Before 1980 they are race categories as reported, because the Bureau did not
ask Hispanic origin of every household until then. Race and Hispanic origin
being separate questions answered by the same people, those earlier categories
can overlap --- in 1970 they sum to about 2.8 per cent more than the county,
and panel (b) rescales that column to 100 per cent.

Hispanic or Latino residents were not counted separately before 1970, nor Asian
and Pacific Islander residents before 1950. Each was small when first counted
--- 1,387 and 57 residents, 0.8 and 0.04 per cent of the county --- so little
is likely to be hidden, though Hispanic residents before 1970 were recorded as
white rather than not recorded at all. Asian and Pacific Islander are reported
together before 2000 and separately after, and are combined throughout so the
series is consistent. ``Other or Multiracial'' is American Indian and Alaska
Native, some other race, and two or more races; before 1980 it is the residents
the four reported categories do not account for, never more than 202 people.

Board members carry one code each, so the Board categories do not overlap and
can be read against the census ones directly: a Hispanic or Latino member
appears in that category whatever their race.

Terms follow the Bureau's own wording throughout.
