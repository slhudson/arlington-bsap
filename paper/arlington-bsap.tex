\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{residents_by_race.pdf}
  \caption{Arlington County residents by race and ethnicity, 1870--2020.
  Panel (a) gives counts, panel (b) each group's share. From 1980 the groups
  are the Census Bureau's categories crossed with Hispanic origin, so they do
  not overlap and sum to the county exactly; groups other than Hispanic or
  Latino are non-Hispanic. Before 1980 they are race categories as reported,
  because the Bureau did not ask Hispanic origin of every household until 1980.
  Terms follow the Bureau's own wording.
  Asian and Pacific Islander are reported together before 2000 and separately
  after; they are combined throughout so the series is consistent. Because
  race and Hispanic origin were separate questions answered by the same people,
  the pre-1980 categories can overlap: in 1970 they sum to about 2.8 per cent
  more than the county total, and panel (b) rescales that column to 100 per
  cent. ``Other or Multiracial'' is American Indian and Alaska Native, some
  other race, and two or more races; before 1980 it is the residents the four
  reported categories do not account for, never more than 202 people.}
  \label{fig:residents-race}
\end{figure}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{board_race.pdf}
  \caption{Composition of the Arlington County Board by race and ethnicity,
  1871--2026, in seats rather than shares. The dashed line marks 1932, when the
  Board expanded from three to five seats and magisterial districts were
  replaced by at-large elections. Half seats occur when a Board member resigned
  or died before the end of the year and was subsequently replaced. 1931 is not
  reported in the source data and appears as a gap. Seats are measured in
  seat-years against the months the Board existed in each year: Arlington's
  Board came into existence at the May 1870 election, so 1870 counts eight
  months rather than twelve. 1873 and 1990 fall below a full Board because
  seats were genuinely vacant. No Asian American or Pacific
  Islander member has served in any year, so no such category is drawn. Each
  member carries one code, so the categories do not overlap and can be read
  against figure~\ref{fig:residents-race} directly: a Hispanic or Latino member
  appears in that category whatever their race, and the others are members who
  are not Hispanic or Latino.}
  \label{fig:board-race}
\end{figure}
