\part{Historical Background}

County Boards in Virginia arguably began in 1869, when the state's post-Civil War
constitution created boards of supervisors for every county in the
commonwealth.\autocite[art.~VII, sec.~2]{vaconstitution1869} The territory now known
as Arlington County was then called Alexandria County, with the neighboring City of
Alexandria to its southeast.\autocite[ch.~39, sec.~2]{vaacts1870} The County was
initially divided into three townships---Washington, Arlington and Jefferson---which
each elected one supervisor to the new Board.\autocite[chs.~39, 76 and 188]{vaacts1870}

Shortly after the Board's creation, in 1874, Virginia voters approved a
constitutional amendment to streamline local government by abolishing the township
model---with its clerks, assessors, collectors and road
overseers.\autocite[ch.~301]{vaacts1873amendment} The County's three townships were renamed
magisterial districts, and each continued to elect one supervisor to the
Board.\autocite{vaacts1875}

This report traces the land the Board governed from 1870 to the present. That
territory has changed in both shape and name over time. The County shrank twice, in
1915 and 1930, when the City of Alexandria annexed sections of its southeast corner,
as depicted in Figure~\ref{fig:residents-district-map}.\autocite{rose1964} In 1920,
the General Assembly renamed what remained Arlington County.

Throughout this report, we define the residents of Arlington County as the people
who lived on the land the Board governed in each year, including the sections now
removed prior to their annexation.
